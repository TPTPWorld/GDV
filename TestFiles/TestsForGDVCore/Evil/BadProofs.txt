While looking at the different cases, I found an incorrect behavior of GDV,
which makes it verifies a (false) proof of

```
fof(a, axiom, p).
fof(b, conjecture, (p & q)).
```

The proof is
```
fof(c_0_0, axiom, (p), file('prob.p', a)).
fof(c_0_1, conjecture, ((p&q)), file('prob.p', b)).
fof(c_0_2, negated_conjecture, ~p, inference(bad_rule,[status(cth)],[c_0_1])).
cnf(c_0_3, negated_conjecture,($false), inference(res,[status(thm)],[c_0_0,
c_0_2]), ['proof']).
```

Indeed, GDV only checks that ~p is a counter theorem of p & q, which is the
case because p & q implies ~~p. In fact, for the proof, we need the
implication in the converse direction. Probably, the most safe approach would
be to check that negated conjectures are counter equivalent to the
conjectures. Most provers (at least those that I looked at) just put a
negation to the conjecture, so it should not be a problem.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
%----One that GDV used to not catch
%----Problem
fof(f1,axiom,p).

fof(f2,conjecture,~p).

%----Bad proof
fof(f1,axiom,p,
    file(input,f1)).

fof(f2,conjecture,~p,
    file(input,f2)).

fof(i1,negated_conjecture,~p,
    inference(broken,[status(thm)],[f2])).

fof(i2,plain,$false,
    inference(resolve,[status(thm)],[f1,i1])).
